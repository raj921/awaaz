What modal run actually does

The command you are about to run: .venv/bin/modal run research/training/train_qwen3_sft.py. Mission: be able to narrate every stage of it in the interview. Reference: pipeline glossary.

The five ideas

1. Defining the App costs nothing

When Modal reads the file, app = modal.App("qwen3-a1-sft") is just a blueprint on your Mac — no cloud, no GPU, no money yet.

train_qwen3_sft.py:17

2. Your Mac runs only the entrypoint

modal run starts at @app.local_entrypoint main() — on your machine. Its whole job is orchestration: call each heavy function with .remote().

3. .remote() teleports the call

First download_base.remote() caches the base model on a cheap CPU container; then train.remote(max_steps=30) ships training to the H100. Your terminal blocks and streams their print()s back — that's why you'll see loss lines live.

4. The container is cooked from the image — once

Image.debian_slim().pip_install("torch", ...) is the recipe. First run cooks it (~10 min: OS + downloads). Later runs reuse the cached image — rebuilds only happen when dependencies change. Inside: an H100 GPU, the mounted MaSaC file, and the Volume at /root/adapter.

train_qwen3_sft.py:18-25, 79

5. The Volume is what survives

Training writes the adapter to the Volume and calls volume.commit(). The container then dies — deliberately. Your adapter lives on the cloud disk, which is why a different container (serving) can read it tomorrow. modal volume get downloads it to your Mac.
